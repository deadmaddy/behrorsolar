START WITH THE PROPERTY

Is Commercial Solar Right for Your Property?

A commercial solar system should not be sized from the monthly bill alone. A meaningful recommendation also considers when electricity is consumed, the connection details, usable roof area, shading and how much generation the property can use directly.

Operating hours, roof type, property ownership and meter ownership can change the design. Planned air conditioning, new equipment or an extension can also change the requirement. These details help establish whether on-grid solar meets the objective or whether a hybrid system needs to be assessed for backup as well.

The starting point is your actual electricity consumption and site conditions, followed by a proposal with clear assumptions.

What we usually need to start

  • A recent electricity bill, including units and connection details
  • Property location
  • Roof type and approximate usable area, if known
  • Operating hours and main daytime loads
  • Owner, tenant or authorised representative status
  • Power-cut pattern and essential backup loads, if required

01 · INSTITUTIONS

Solar for Schools, Colleges & Educational Institutions

Daytime classes can overlap with solar generation, but being open during daylight does not automatically establish economic viability. Actual bills, the academic calendar and the loads connected to each meter still matter.

A campus may have classrooms, computer labs, administration blocks, HVAC, pumps and hostels on different connections. Holidays can reduce daytime consumption while hostel or security loads continue. The assessment needs to distinguish these patterns before choosing capacity.

RCC and metal roofs need a review of structural condition, shading and safe access. Installation and maintenance routes should be considered alongside the institution’s timetable and capital-approval process.

For Tata hybrid solar, identify the circuits that need backup separately from the campus’s total connected load. The inverter and battery must be assessed against those selected loads.

Questions we would ask before recommending a system

  • Which meter supplies the main daytime loads?
  • How does consumption change during holidays and examinations?
  • Are labs, offices, hostels and pumps on separate meters?
  • Who owns the roof, and is safe maintenance access available?
  • Which equipment needs backup, and for how long?
  • Who approves the investment and any planned expansion?

02 · HOSPITALITY

Solar for Hotels & Hospitality Businesses

Two hotels with similar monthly bills can require different solar designs because their load timing and roof conditions can be very different.

Guest-room air conditioning may peak after sunset, while kitchens, laundry, pumps and common services can consume electricity during the day. Banquet bookings and seasonal occupancy can create further variation. Lifts, lighting and multiple buildings also need to be included in the load review.

An on-grid proposal should explain the relationship between daytime generation and actual consumption. A Tata hybrid assessment should separately define essential circuits, required backup duration and the starting requirements of equipment such as pumps. Battery storage should not be presented as automatic backup for every room, lift or air conditioner.

Information useful for a hotel solar assessment

  • Recent bills and seasonal consumption changes
  • Property type, occupancy pattern and banquet operating hours
  • Main daytime and evening loads
  • Roof type, usable area and rooftop equipment
  • Existing backup arrangement and priority circuits
  • Additional rooms or other planned expansion

03 · SMEs

Commercial Solar for SMEs

Offices, shops, showrooms, clinics, workshops and service businesses need a system matched to their working day. A daytime office and an evening-focused retail space can have different requirements even when their total consumption is similar.

Review how much electricity is used during solar hours, whether the roof is owned or leased, the connection constraints and plans for new equipment. Expansion should be considered in the layout and electrical design; it should not be assumed to be possible without checking equipment compatibility and available space.

If power cuts interrupt billing, communications or other essential work, assess those loads for a Tata hybrid system. The proposal should identify what the backup circuit will support and the conditions that affect runtime.

Is a bigger solar system always better?

No. A larger system is not automatically better. The useful system size depends on load, roof, connection and the economics of self-consumption versus exported electricity.

Adding capacity without reviewing these factors can increase the investment without producing the same increase in useful electricity benefit.

04 · COMMERCIAL RENTAL PROPERTY

Solar for Commercial Rental Properties

Before sizing solar, establish three things: who owns the roof, whose name is on the electricity connection, and who pays the electricity bill.

A landlord-owned roof may sit above tenant-paid meters, an owner-occupied office and a separate common-area connection. The assessment must identify which connection the proposal concerns and who is intended to receive the electricity benefit.

Lifts, water pumps, parking lighting and shared services can be relevant common-area loads. Their operating times still need to be reviewed. A solar installation should not be assumed to offset every meter in the building.

Before proceeding, clarify roof access, owner approval, lease duration, who funds the installation and who arranges maintenance. Future tenant changes and shared roof use also belong in the discussion. Any owner–tenant arrangements need to be settled by the parties before the technical proposal is finalised.

Common commercial-property scenarios

Landlord’s common-area meter

Assess the bill and daytime use for shared services. Identify whether the objective is on-grid generation, selected backup loads or both.

Long-term tenant

Review the tenant’s connection, owner approval and available roof rights alongside the expected occupancy period and maintenance responsibilities.

Partly owner-occupied building

Separate the owner’s consumption from tenant and common-area loads. Establish which meter and roof area the proposed system will use.

Multiple tenants

Map the meters and shared roof before discussing capacity. Any arrangement involving more than one connection needs its own assessment.

Is the solar investment intended to reduce the owner’s common-area bill, the tenant’s electricity cost, or both?

FROM BILL TO PROPOSAL

How Global Green Energy Assesses a Commercial Solar Project

Capacity follows the assessment of the property, its connection and electricity use.

  1. Electricity bill review

    Review units consumed, connection and tariff details, and the billing pattern. More than one bill helps explain seasonal changes.

  2. Understanding daytime load

    Discuss operating hours, major equipment and the balance between daytime and evening use. For hybrid solar, record essential backup loads and the outage pattern.

  3. Roof and site review

    Assess usable area, roof type, shading, obstructions and access. Keep space for existing rooftop equipment and future maintenance.

  4. Connection and electrical review

    Identify the relevant meter, supply arrangement, distribution boards and cable routes. Establish practical electrical constraints before proposing capacity.

  5. System sizing

    Match solar capacity to the available evidence. Where backup is needed, size inverter power and usable battery energy separately from the solar array.

  6. Equipment and design

    Define the Tata system configuration, mounting approach, protection and monitoring. Record exact equipment models and battery details in the proposal.

  7. Commercial proposal

    Set out scope, assumptions, exclusions and payment stages. Explain any dependencies that must be resolved before installation.

Tata On-Grid or Tata Hybrid: Which Fits Your Business?

Both options begin with the same bill and site review. The additional question for hybrid solar is what must continue operating when the grid fails.

Tata Power Solaroof on-grid

Solar generation supplies the property while the grid is available. Surplus energy is handled under the applicable approved connection and billing arrangement.

Power cuts: a standard grid-tied inverter shuts down for safety when grid supply fails. On-grid solar alone does not provide backup.

Assessment focus: electricity use, daytime consumption, roof, connection and project economics.

Tata Power Solaroof hybrid

A compatible hybrid system coordinates solar, grid supply and battery storage. Selected circuits can receive backup within the designed inverter and battery limits.

Power cuts: supported loads and runtime depend on usable battery energy, sunlight, settings and actual demand. Whole-building backup is not assumed.

Assessment focus: all on-grid factors, plus essential loads, starting currents, backup duration and battery placement.

The commercial options on this page are Tata Power Solaroof systems. Exact component models, ratings and warranty terms belong in the site-specific proposal. For the basic operating differences, read On-Grid vs Hybrid vs Off-Grid Solar.

UNDERSTAND THE ASSUMPTIONS

What Determines Commercial Solar Economics?

There is no single payback period that applies to every commercial solar project.

The financial comparison depends on how much generation is used on site, the applicable electricity tariff and export treatment, the project cost and the property’s future electricity use. Generation estimates need stated assumptions about the selected equipment, orientation and shading.

Usable roof area, mounting requirements and cable routes affect project design and cost. Financing costs, maintenance and any expected replacement costs also need to be accounted for where relevant. An on-grid proposal and a hybrid proposal should show their different scopes clearly, including the purpose and cost of battery backup.

Compare the assumptions as well as the final amount. Residential subsidy figures should not be carried into a commercial proposal.

CHECK THE WHOLE INSTALLATION

Commercial Roof & Site Assessment

Roof condition and usable area

RCC, metal-sheet and mixed roofs need site-specific review. The gross roof area does not equal the area available for modules. Check structural condition, mounting requirements and access before finalising the layout.

Shading and obstructions

Water tanks, nearby buildings, parapets and rooftop equipment can affect the layout. Keep service access to these features and consider how shadows change across the roof.

Electrical installation

Review inverter location, cable routes, earthing and electrical protection. For a hybrid system, include battery placement and the separation of designated backup circuits.

Maintenance access

Plan safe access for cleaning, inspection and servicing. A densely filled roof is not a complete design if routine maintenance cannot be carried out safely.

COMPARE THE SCOPE

What a Serious Commercial Solar Proposal Should Show

Comparing only the final rupee amount can hide differences in equipment, installation work and responsibilities. A proposal should identify the following, where applicable.

System and equipment

  • Proposed DC capacity and inverter capacity
  • Module and inverter make and model
  • Mounting structure and cable scope
  • AC/DC protection, earthing and monitoring
  • For hybrid: battery model, usable energy, backup circuits and operating limits

Responsibilities and commercial terms

  • Installation and workmanship scope
  • Approval and net-metering responsibilities, where applicable
  • Equipment warranties and who handles each responsibility
  • Payment stages and exclusions
  • Generation assumptions and site-specific dependencies

PRACTICAL QUESTIONS

Commercial Solar FAQs

Is commercial solar eligible for PM Surya Ghar subsidy?

The PM Surya Ghar residential subsidy does not apply to commercial connections. Commercial proposals should be assessed without residential CFA assumptions. See our PM Surya Ghar guide for residential scheme information.

How is a commercial solar system sized?

A commercial system should not be sized from the monthly bill alone. Global Green Energy considers consumption, daytime load, connection details, usable roof, shading, ownership and future requirements before recommending a capacity. For hybrid solar, the backup load and battery requirement are assessed separately.

How much roof area does commercial solar require?

There is no single roof-area figure that fits every project. The requirement depends on the proposed capacity, module dimensions, mounting layout, shading, obstructions and maintenance access. A survey establishes usable area before the layout is finalised.

Can I install solar on a rented commercial property?

A rented property can be assessed, but roof ownership, owner approval, electricity connection, lease duration and maintenance access must be clarified. Establish who funds the system and who receives the electricity benefit before finalising the proposal.

Can a landlord use solar for common-area electricity?

A common-area connection can be assessed for loads such as pumps, lifts and shared lighting. Its consumption pattern, meter details and roof availability determine the proposed design. A system should not be assumed to offset separate tenant meters.

Is rooftop solar suitable for schools and colleges?

It can be worth assessing where electricity is consumed during solar hours, but the bills and load profile still need review. Academic holidays, multiple meters, labs, HVAC, hostels, roof condition and safe maintenance access can all affect the recommendation.

Is solar suitable for hotels?

Suitability depends on the hotel’s actual consumption and roof. Daytime kitchen, laundry, cooling and pumping loads can differ from evening guest-room or banquet demand. Seasonal occupancy and any backup requirement should be included in the assessment.

Can commercial solar be installed on metal-sheet roofs?

Metal-sheet roofs can be considered after a site-specific review of structural condition, mounting, weatherproofing, access and electrical routes. The presence of a large metal roof alone does not establish that it is suitable.

What information do you need for a commercial solar proposal?

Start with a recent electricity bill, property location, roof type, operating hours, owner or tenant status and connection details. Include planned load growth and, for hybrid solar, the essential loads and backup duration you want assessed.

Does commercial solar require a site survey?

Yes. Bills and photographs help with the initial discussion, but the roof, shading, structural condition, cable routes, inverter location and electrical connection need a site review before the design and scope are finalised.

Will Tata on-grid solar work during a power cut?

A standard on-grid inverter shuts down when the grid fails, even in daylight. Backup requires a suitably designed hybrid system with compatible equipment, battery storage and selected backup circuits.

Does Tata hybrid solar provide backup for the entire building?

Whole-building backup should not be assumed. The inverter’s output and starting-load capability, usable battery energy, sunlight and system settings determine which circuits can run and for how long. The proposal should state the supported loads.

How long does commercial solar installation take?

The schedule depends on the roof, system scope, equipment availability, site access and any required approvals. Installation work and external approval steps should be discussed separately; one fixed timeline does not apply to every property.
