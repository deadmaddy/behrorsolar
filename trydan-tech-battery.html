TRYDAN TECH 100Ah LiFePO₄ battery review: price, unboxing & Zelio charging test

Actual purchase price, package contents and initial charging observations with a 2017 Luminous Zelio 1100/12V inverter.

By Behror Solar · 3 November 2026 · Ongoing real-world review

Can a nine-year-old home inverter designed for lead-acid batteries charge a modern lithium iron phosphate battery?

We purchased a TRYDAN TECH 12.8V 100Ah LiFePO₄ battery for ₹19,100, including GST and transportation, and began testing it with a 2017 Luminous Zelio 1100/12V inverter. Of the total paid, ₹1,400 was charged for transportation and ₹17,700 for the battery, including GST.

The package contained only the battery and terminal screws. No additional accessories or technical documentation were supplied.

These figures represent our actual purchase experience and should not be interpreted as the manufacturer's standard retail price.

In this independent, ongoing review, we're documenting everything from the actual purchase price and unboxing experience to charging voltages, inverter compatibility, backup runtime and usable battery capacity.

Our first charging observations are encouraging. The battery's terminal voltage rose from 13.21V out of the box to a maximum observed 14.00V during charging, before decreasing to 13.80V while grid power remained ON.

Here's everything we've documented so far.

Unboxing: What's Included in the Package?

The package we received was minimal.

Inside the box were:

  1. One TRYDAN TECH 12.8V 100Ah LiFePO₄ battery
  2. Battery terminal screws

No other accessories or documentation were included in our package.

In particular, we did not receive a printed charging guide or a detailed model-specific BMS specification sheet.

That missing information matters because the recommended charging voltage, allowable charging current, continuous discharge current, surge rating and BMS protection thresholds are important when pairing a lithium battery with an older inverter.

Although TRYDAN informed us that the battery would work with our Luminous Zelio, we still intend to obtain its detailed technical parameters.

Our unboxing assessment: The package provided the battery and basic terminal hardware, but model-specific technical documentation would have been useful for installation and compatibility verification.

This describes the contents of our particular purchase. Packaging and accessories supplied to other buyers may differ.

Why We're Testing It with a 2017 Luminous Zelio 1100

Our test inverter is a Luminous Zelio 1100/12V dating from 2017.

Unlike newer inverters with dedicated lithium battery profiles, this unit has conventional lead-acid battery selection modes.

TRYDAN informed us that its lithium battery would work with the Zelio.

We therefore wanted to observe the actual battery-terminal voltage during grid-powered charging instead of simply assuming the two products were fully compatible.

Our tests are being conducted using grid electricity, not solar-panel charging.

Out-of-Box Voltage: 13.21V

Before connecting the battery, we measured its voltage using a digital multimeter.

Result: 13.21V DC

After connecting the battery to the Zelio, with grid power OFF and no external load, the reading remained 13.21V.

This became our initial baseline.

A 13.21V reading is plausible for a four-cell LiFePO₄ battery, but it cannot reliably indicate its remaining charge percentage because LiFePO₄ batteries have a relatively flat voltage curve across much of their usable capacity.

Initial Charging Test: Voltage Begins Rising

After switching grid power ON, we monitored the battery-terminal voltage with no external appliances connected.

Within approximately two minutes, voltage reached 13.40V.

At approximately five minutes, the reading was 13.43V.

Subsequent observations showed 13.45V and 13.55V.

After Approximately Five Hours: 14.00V

Around 3 AM on 3 November, after approximately five hours of charging, the voltage reached 14.00V.

A repeated reading showed 13.98V.

These measurements suggested that the charger was operating near 14V during that period.

The battery casing also remained cool to the touch, with no noticeable overheating reported.

However, we cannot establish a full battery charge merely from terminal voltage or casing temperature.

Charging current, battery state of charge and the manufacturer's approved charging limits remain important missing measurements.

Voltage Decreased to 13.80V While Grid Remained ON

The most interesting result came after the earlier 14.00V observations.

At approximately 4 AM, the terminal voltage measured 13.82V.

By 4:15 AM, it had decreased slightly further to 13.80V.

Throughout these observations, grid power remained ON and no external load was connected.

This indicates that the battery-terminal voltage decreased by approximately 0.20V from the highest observed reading while mains power remained available.

One possible explanation is that the Zelio transitioned from a higher-voltage charging stage toward a lower-voltage maintenance stage.

However, we cannot confirm actual float mode without charging-current data and additional testing.

The accurate finding is that the inverter exhibited a reduction in battery-terminal voltage after the earlier near-14V charging period.

Did the Zelio Consider the Battery Fully Charged?

During the early charging period, the inverter displayed an estimated 40 minutes remaining until full charge.

Later, this estimated time disappeared from its display.

This suggests that the Zelio's own charge-estimation system may have reached a condition associated with charging completion.

However, the Zelio was designed around lead-acid battery characteristics, which differ from LiFePO₄ batteries.

The disappearance of the countdown does not establish that the lithium battery was actually at 100% state of charge.

We have not yet obtained an independent battery state-of-charge reading.

Why Voltage Dropped to 13.30V When the Grid Was Switched OFF

During one of the initial observations, the battery-terminal voltage dropped immediately from approximately 13.45V with mains ON to 13.30V after grid power was switched OFF.

This is consistent with the applied charging voltage being removed.

It does not indicate that a substantial amount of stored energy disappeared instantly.

The 13.30V reading also cannot reliably be converted into a charge percentage because it was taken immediately after charging stopped.

Future testing will aim to establish the actual available battery capacity using more appropriate measurement methods.

Initial Findings: What We've Verified

Our current observations support the following conclusions:

  • The battery measured 13.21V directly out of the box.
  • The Zelio applied charging voltage to the battery during the initial test.
  • The highest observed charging voltage was 14.00V.
  • The battery's casing remained cool to the touch during later charging observations.
  • The Zelio stopped displaying its charging-time estimate.
  • Battery-terminal voltage decreased to 13.80V while grid power remained ON.

These are observations from our particular battery and inverter combination.

They do not establish full charging compatibility, battery capacity, or long-term operating safety.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the price of a TRYDAN TECH 100Ah lithium battery in India?

Our TRYDAN TECH 12.8V 100Ah LiFePO₄ battery cost ₹19,100, including GST and transportation. This is our actual purchase price, not a guaranteed nationwide selling price.

What comes inside the TRYDAN battery box?

Our package contained the battery and terminal screws. No other accessories or technical documentation were included in the package we received.

Can a 100Ah LiFePO₄ battery work with a Luminous Zelio 1100?

TRYDAN informed us that its battery was compatible with our Zelio. Our initial measurements show that the inverter applied charging voltage, but long-term compatibility and correct charging behaviour still require verification.

Is 14.00V normal for a 12.8V lithium battery?

14.00V falls within a commonly used charging range for four-cell LiFePO₄ batteries. However, the manufacturer's own limits determine the permitted operating conditions.

Does 13.30V mean the battery is 90% charged?

Not necessarily. LiFePO₄ voltage varies with charging, rest and discharge conditions, and a single voltage measurement is not a reliable state-of-charge indicator.

Is 1.28kWh the usable capacity?

No. It is the battery's nominal energy rating. Usable capacity depends on operating conditions, discharge limits and actual battery performance, none of which have yet been independently established in this review.

Our Initial Verdict

For ₹19,100 including GST and transportation, we received a TRYDAN TECH 12.8V 100Ah LiFePO₄ battery with terminal screws and no additional accessories or model-specific technical documentation.

During our initial test with a 2017 Luminous Zelio 1100, charging voltage rose gradually from 13.21V to a highest observed 14.00V.

It later decreased to 13.80V while grid power remained ON.

What looks promising: Initial charging-voltage behaviour and no noticeable casing heating.

What needs improvement: Availability of clear model-specific charging and BMS specifications.

What remains unverified: Actual usable capacity, backup runtime, charging efficiency, long-term compatibility and durability.

Final verdict: Pending further testing.

We will continue updating this same review with verified findings rather than publishing disconnected reports for every new reading.
