THE QUESTION

Same 5 kW solar array. Why does daily generation change?

A rooftop solar system's installed capacity remains the same throughout the year.

But its electricity production does not.

As October transitions into November in northern Rajasthan, daylight hours shorten, the sun's seasonal position changes, and environmental conditions begin shifting towards winter.

There is an interesting contradiction here.

Cooler temperatures can improve solar panel efficiency. Yet improved efficiency does not necessarily mean higher daily energy production.

So what happens to a real 5 kWp rooftop installation during this seasonal transition?

Rather than relying on a brochure or an estimated generation chart, we examined 31 days of actual inverter-recorded solar production from a Tata rooftop installation in the Alwar–Behror region.

This is a field observation from one operating system, not a controlled scientific experiment or a forecast of winter generation across Rajasthan.

That variation is an important part of understanding real-world rooftop solar performance.

How the numbers are calculated

Average daily production

616.50 kWh ÷ 31 days = 19.89 kWh/day

Specific energy yield

616.50 kWh ÷ 5 kWp = 123.30 kWh/kWp

Average daily specific yield

123.30 kWh/kWp ÷ 31 days = 3.98 kWh/kWp/day

Specific yield allows the recorded energy production to be expressed relative to installed solar capacity.

This is particularly useful when examining systems of different sizes or comparing future monitoring periods.

However, specific yield alone is not the same as performance ratio. A formal performance-ratio calculation would require additional information, including solar irradiance.

THE SEASONAL TRANSITION

Why winter can change solar production even when panels become cooler

A solar panel's electricity production depends on more than temperature.

During the transition from October into November, several factors can influence the amount of electricity produced in the Alwar–Behror region.

1. Shorter days mean a changing solar generation window

As northern Rajasthan moves towards the December winter solstice, the number of daylight hours decreases.

Even if a solar panel operates efficiently in cooler weather, a shorter period of available sunlight can reduce the total energy it generates during the day.

This is one reason why panel efficiency and daily electricity production should not be treated as interchangeable concepts.

2. Fixed panel angles interact with seasonal sun positions

Most residential rooftop solar systems use fixed mounting structures.

The panels remain at their installed tilt and orientation throughout the year.

But the sun's position changes with the seasons.

As winter approaches, the sun follows a lower path across the sky. Depending on the direction and angle of the panels, this changes how sunlight strikes the module surface.

A fixed-angle installation can perform effectively throughout the year, but its output naturally varies with seasonal solar geometry.

The precise impact depends on the actual mounting angle, orientation, roof location, and surrounding environment.

3. Fog, haze and atmospheric dust can affect sunlight availability

The Alwar–Behror region experiences changing atmospheric conditions as winter approaches.

Haze, airborne dust, and fog can reduce the amount of direct solar radiation reaching rooftop panels.

Diffuse sunlight may still produce electricity, but daily energy output depends on the total irradiance reaching the array.

Not every low-generation day is necessarily a foggy day.

Without matching weather observations to the production records, fog and haze remain possible explanations rather than confirmed causes of a particular day's output.

4. Longer winter shadows can affect rooftop generation

A lower sun angle can increase the length of shadows cast by nearby buildings, trees, water tanks, parapet walls, and other obstacles.

A roof that receives little shading during summer may experience different shading patterns in winter.

This makes seasonal shading assessment important when designing rooftop solar installations.

The actual installation's winter shading has not been separately measured in this observation.

5. Grid availability is another important variable

For a conventional grid-connected solar system, electricity production is affected by grid availability.

When the grid supply fails, a standard on-grid inverter generally disconnects and stops supplying electricity, even if sunlight is available.

This is a safety requirement known as anti-islanding protection.

Consequently, an interruption in grid supply can reduce the energy recorded by an inverter during an otherwise sunny day.

Our 31-day dataset does not include a verified grid-outage log, so we cannot calculate the contribution of grid interruptions to the observed production variations.

6. Cooler temperatures can improve conversion efficiency

There is also a potentially favourable factor.

Solar modules generally experience lower temperature-related power losses when their operating temperatures decrease.

This can improve conversion efficiency compared with hotter conditions, assuming other factors are comparable.

However, the benefit of cooler modules must be considered alongside shorter daylight hours, changing irradiation angles, atmospheric conditions, and system availability.

A cooler solar panel does not automatically mean a higher daily energy yield.

MYTH VS REALITY

Does a 5 kW solar system always generate 20–25 units per day?

Myth: A 5 kW rooftop solar system should generate approximately the same number of units every day, regardless of season.

Reality: The 5 kWp rating represents the array's nominal DC capacity under standard test conditions, not a promise of fixed daily electricity production.

Daily generation depends on sunlight availability, mounting geometry, module temperature, shading, inverter operation, grid availability, and other site-specific conditions.

The observed 31-day average of 19.89 units is a measured result for this installation and period.

It should not be interpreted as a guaranteed daily output for every 5 kW system in Rajasthan.

WHAT THE DATA DOES — AND DOESN'T — SHOW

One month provides evidence, not an annual performance guarantee

The 616.50 kWh result tells us how much solar electricity the inverter recorded over the monitoring period.

The daily graph also establishes that actual output varied from day to day.

However, these observations alone cannot prove that winter was responsible for any particular change in production.

To isolate the causes of daily variation, we would need additional evidence, including:

  • Daily solar irradiance and weather observations.
  • Grid outage and inverter event logs.
  • Module orientation and tilt details.
  • Site-specific shading observations.
  • Comparable monitoring data from other seasons.

The distinction matters.

A reduction in sunlight, an extended shadow, a period of fog, or an inverter shutdown can all influence the day's recorded energy.

The production total does not independently reveal which factor was responsible.

We therefore treat this 31-day period as an early-winter transition observation, not a complete winter performance study.

FOUR QUESTIONS FOR SOLAR OWNERS

What should you check when solar generation changes in winter?

1. Are you monitoring actual daily production?

An inverter monitoring application can provide useful information about how much solar electricity is being produced each day.

Reviewing daily and monthly records helps establish a baseline for your particular rooftop.

2. Has the shading pattern changed?

Observe whether buildings, trees, water tanks, or parapet walls cast longer shadows over the panels as the sun's seasonal position changes.

3. Were there grid outages or inverter interruptions?

A low-generation day does not automatically indicate a panel problem.

Check available inverter event records or electricity-supply interruptions before drawing conclusions.

4. Are you comparing equivalent periods?

Comparing October–November production directly with peak-summer generation can be misleading without considering seasonal differences.

A stronger assessment uses records from comparable periods and, ideally, multiple years.

Solar performance should be measured, not assumed

At BehrorSolar.in, our approach is to make rooftop solar information more useful through actual field observations.

The 5 kWp Tata installation examined here produced 616.50 kWh over 31 days, averaging 19.89 kWh per day.

Those figures provide a real production reference for one installation during the transition towards winter in the Alwar–Behror region.

They do not establish a regional average, guarantee future generation, or reveal the causes of every daily fluctuation.

For homeowners in Behror, Neemrana, Alwar, and surrounding areas, the broader lesson is straightforward.

Understanding solar generation requires looking beyond installed capacity and theoretical daily-unit estimates.

Site design, panel orientation, seasonal sunlight, shading, equipment operation and grid reliability all matter.

A solar array's installed capacity stays the same across seasons. The conditions under which it generates electricity do not.

That is why we believe real generation monitoring should remain part of the conversation long after a rooftop solar system has been installed.

HOW TO READ THE EVIDENCE

Production data, not household electricity consumption

The solar production figures in this article come from the system's inverter monitoring application.

The installation does not have Wi-Fi/IoT-enabled energy meters providing independently verified readings for household electricity consumption, grid imports, or grid exports.

Accordingly:

  • 616.50 kWh refers to inverter-recorded solar production.
  • Actual household electricity consumption has not been established.
  • The amount of solar electricity consumed within the property has not been measured.
  • Grid electricity imports and exports have not been independently measured.
  • Electricity bill savings cannot be calculated from these production figures alone.

Any consumption, import, or export fields displayed in the monitoring app should not be treated as verified measurements without the necessary metering hardware and data.

The observations are specific to this 5 kWp installation and the stated dates.

They do not constitute independent laboratory testing, a formal performance-ratio assessment, or an annual generation forecast.

COMMON QUESTIONS

5 kW solar generation in Rajasthan — quick answers

How many units did the monitored 5 kW Tata solar system generate?

The 5 kWp DC installation recorded 616.50 kWh of solar electricity production between 13 October and 12 November 2026, over 31 consecutive days.

What was the average daily solar generation?

The average was approximately 19.89 kWh per day. Individual daily results varied, so this should not be interpreted as a fixed production figure.

Does solar generation decrease during winter in Rajasthan?

It can. Shorter daylight hours, lower sun angles, changing shading and reduced sunlight from fog or haze may lower energy production. Cooler module temperatures can partly counteract these effects by improving conversion efficiency. The net result depends on local conditions and system configuration.

Can fog affect rooftop solar generation in Behror and Neemrana?

Yes. Fog can reduce available solar irradiance and affect electricity production. However, confirming the impact on a particular day requires weather information alongside the inverter's generation data.

Does a fixed solar panel tilt reduce winter generation?

The effect depends on the actual tilt, orientation, solar position and roof conditions. Fixed-tilt systems do not adjust automatically with the changing seasons, so their generation profile changes throughout the year. A fixed tilt is not necessarily unsuitable for winter.

Does a grid outage affect rooftop solar production?

A conventional on-grid inverter generally stops operating when grid electricity is unavailable. The behaviour of hybrid systems can differ according to their design, backup configuration and operating mode.

Is 616.50 units the electricity consumed by the house?

No. This is the amount of electricity recorded as produced by the solar inverter. Household consumption, grid import, grid export and solar self-consumption were not independently measured.

Can this data predict the annual generation of a 5 kW solar system?

Not reliably. A single 31-day period cannot establish annual output. Seasonal changes, local weather, system losses, outages and other operating conditions must be considered when estimating yearly generation.
