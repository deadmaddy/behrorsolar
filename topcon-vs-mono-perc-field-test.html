TOPCon vs Mono PERC: We Put Both on One Roof and Measured

Same roof. Same reflective coating. Same monsoon sky. The TOPCon array recorded 22.46 kWh and the Mono PERC array 16.7 kWh — and when we measured again six days later, the gap held. Here is what we observed, what the numbers do and do not prove, and what they may mean if you're choosing panels in Behror or Neemrana.

By Mahendra Yadav · Behror Solar · Updated 27 August 2026

The question

Brochures argue. We wanted to measure.

The TOPCon-versus-Mono-PERC debate is usually fought on spec sheets. Every manufacturer's brochure claims the edge. So instead of trusting a brochure, we did the simple thing: we ran the two panel technologies side by side on the same rooftop and read the meters.

This isn't a lab study. It's a working-installer field note from a real roof in the Behror–Neemrana belt — the kind of honest data we wish we'd had when we started fitting solar.

The setup

Two arrays observed on the same roof

Both arrays sat on the same roof, with the same white reflective coating underneath, under the same monsoon sky, on the same day. This reduced several environmental differences, but it was not a controlled laboratory test. Module model, electrical configuration, mounting and measurement conditions may also have affected the result.

The raw gap is 22.46 versus 16.7 kWh — about 34.5% more energy. One electricity “unit” equals one kilowatt-hour (kWh). The raw percentage is not a fair technology comparison because Array A also had more installed capacity.

The honest math

On this test day, TOPCon produced 16.9% more per installed kWp

Part of the 34.5% raw gap comes from the higher-wattage TOPCon modules (610 W vs 530 W), which made Array A larger. Normalising by installed capacity gives a more useful comparison:

TOPCon recorded 6.14 kWh/kWp; Mono PERC recorded 5.25 kWh/kWp. That is approximately 16.9% more energy per installed kWp on this test day. It is an observed difference between these two arrays, not proof that cell technology alone caused the entire gap.

If you hear a raw percentage for TOPCon versus Mono PERC, check the installed capacities. For these arrays, the more useful normalised observation is 16.9% more per installed kWp on this test day—with the limitations described below.

The repeat test

We measured again on 27 August — the same result

A single day can be a fluke — a passing cloud, a dusty panel, an odd weather hour. So we ran the same two arrays again six days later, on 27 August 2026. The TOPCon array recorded 22.16 kWh and the Mono PERC array 16.4 kWh — almost identical to the 21 August figures. Normalised by installed capacity, that is again about 17% more per installed kWp for the TOPCon array. Two separate days, days apart, and the gap held.

This still isn't a controlled or year-long study — but a result that repeats on two separate days is a good deal harder to dismiss as a one-day fluke than a single reading would be. We'll keep logging as the seasons change.

Possible explanations

Why TOPCon may perform differently

This test did not measure irradiance, rear-side contribution or module temperature, so it cannot isolate the cause of the difference. Two characteristics may have contributed and are worth checking against the exact module datasheets:

Monsoon · possible factor

Low light + bifacial gain

Diffuse light and rear-side reflection from the white roof may have influenced output. The size of that effect depends on the particular modules, roof geometry, mounting height and rear-side irradiance; this test did not measure those contributions separately.

Summer · what to expect

Lower heat loss

Many TOPCon modules have a less-negative power temperature coefficient than older PERC modules, which can reduce heat-related losses. The actual advantage must be calculated from the two module datasheets and operating temperatures.

A longer, season-by-season log is needed before estimating the annual difference between these particular arrays.

What it means for you

This isn't "one brand beats another"

A rooftop system is a 25-year investment, and that decision deserves measured data rather than marketing. Here's the balanced read:

In this observation, the higher-wattage TOPCon bifacial array generated 16.9% more per installed kWp than the PERC array. That makes it a promising performance-and-roof-space option, but the buying decision should also compare installed price, module efficiency, product and performance warranties, service support, inverter compatibility and expected lifetime generation.

TOPCon and PERC are technologies; Tata Power is a brand. A brand may offer modules using either technology, so compare the exact model and datasheet—not just the brand name or cell label.

The honest caveat

Two days is a stronger signal, not a season

These are two monsoon-day observations, from 21 and 27 August 2026 — a repeatable signal, but still not a controlled or year-long study. Each array used a separate inverter; the inverter brands are intentionally not identified because this comparison is about the observed arrays, not inverter promotion. The TOPCon monitor briefly displayed about 4.2 kW, but that instantaneous value still needs its sampling interval and AC/DC basis documented before it can be interpreted confidently. We are logging more days across seasons before estimating an annual difference.

Common questions

TOPCon vs Mono PERC — quick answers

Does TOPCon always beat Mono PERC?

No. This was a comparison between two differently sized arrays on one roof, measured on two monsoon days (21 and 27 August 2026). Results vary by module model, inverter and MPPT configuration, mounting, shading, weather and season. In this observation, the TOPCon array recorded about 17% more energy per installed kWp on both days.

Is TOPCon eligible for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?

Yes — when it's a DCR (made-in-India) TOPCon module. We fit DCR-certified TOPCon panels that qualify for the full PM Surya Ghar subsidy, the same as any eligible system.

Should I compare TOPCon with a brand such as Tata Power?

Compare the exact module models rather than treating a technology and a brand as opposites. Review efficiency, temperature coefficient, bifacial rating, warranties, service support, installed price and expected generation for your roof. We can help you compare those figures model by model.
