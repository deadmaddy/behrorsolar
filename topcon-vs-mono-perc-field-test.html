TOPCon vs Mono PERC: quality matters more than the lowest quote

A low upfront price can hide a more expensive trade-off: less electricity generated over time. Our September records compare a budget Mono PERC installation with a premium TOPCon array on the same roof. A separate Tata Mono PERC record shows why high-quality PERC can still stand alongside newer TOPCon.

By Mahendra Yadav · Behror Solar · Updated 9 October 2026

The long-term question

A cheaper quote can become a more expensive choice

A solar system is a long-term investment. Its value depends on the electricity it generates, the quality of its equipment and installation, and the support behind it. If a lower-cost system produces less electricity, it can offset less of your grid consumption. The initial saving can then be eroded over time.

That is why we compare actual generation alongside the quotation. And it is why high-quality Mono PERC and premium TOPCon both belong on the shortlist. The technology name alone does not tell you whether a system is good value.

From 1 September to 30 September 2026, the premium TOPCon array and the budget Mono PERC array were observed on the same Behror rooftop. These are the same two arrays used in our August field test.

For these two arrays, the observed September difference was 19.3% more energy per installed DC kWp for TOPCon. The budget installation's lower recorded output is the long-term-value concern: a lower quote needs to be weighed against how much electricity the complete system actually produces.

How the September comparison is calculated

Rated DC capacity: six 610 W panels = 3.66 kWp; six 530 W panels = 3.18 kWp. Recorded energy divided by capacity: 529.13 ÷ 3.66 = 144.57 kWh/kWp, and 385.4 ÷ 3.18 = 121.19 kWh/kWp. The percentage difference is calculated from the unrounded values.

One electricity unit equals 1 kWh. Daily averages divide each monthly total by 30. These are recorded period figures; they are not annual generation or lifetime savings estimates.

Quality Mono PERC remains competitive

High-quality Tata Mono PERC can stand alongside newer TOPCon

The budget 530 W Mono PERC array is one specific installation. It should not represent every Mono PERC system. Our separate Tata example in Alwar City uses six 590 W Mono PERC panels and recorded 521.1 units in 30 days, averaging 17.4 units a day.

The TOPCon record shows 17.64 units a day over its September period. Both are strong recorded results. They illustrate why a high-quality Mono PERC module can be a competitive option alongside new-generation TOPCon.

The Tata installation is in a different city and covers 9 September–8 October, while the paired rooftop records cover 1–30 September. Read these as dated examples of system output. They do not establish a matched Tata-versus-TOPCon winner.

Earlier observations · the same two arrays

The August readings showed the same direction

Before September's full-month record, we measured these same TOPCon and budget Mono PERC arrays on 21 August and 27 August 2026. TOPCon recorded 16.9% and 17.4% more energy per rated DC kWp on those respective days.

Both arrays used the same roof, white reflective coating, tilt, direction, mounting height and shading conditions. They used separate inverters. The monthly log adds a longer observation of these installations; it remains a field record rather than a controlled laboratory study.

What to compare before choosing

Buy long-term value, not just the lowest price

A cheaper quotation only saves money if the complete system still delivers the performance and support you need. Compare these points alongside the upfront price:

  • Expected generation: use the actual panel-array capacity and a realistic expectation for your roof.
  • Exact equipment: compare the module model, inverter and electrical configuration.
  • Installation quality: look at the mounting structure, wiring, earthing and site design.
  • Warranty and service: understand the product and performance terms, responsibility and local support.

The choice can be high-quality Tata Mono PERC or premium TOPCon. A technology label should not outweigh the quality and value of the complete installation. Lower-cost solar can cost more over time when the upfront saving comes with lower output or weaker support.

How to read the evidence

Recorded results, with the conditions kept visible

The paired TOPCon and budget Mono PERC arrays used different DC capacities and separate inverters. Irradiance, rear-side contribution, module temperature and detailed MPPT/electrical conditions were not measured, so the records cannot isolate cell technology as the sole cause of the difference. Tata's Alwar example has a different location and date range. Output varies with weather, season, orientation, shading, grid availability, equipment and site conditions. These observations are not an annual generation guarantee or a lifetime financial calculation.

Additional measurement note from the original field test

The TOPCon monitor briefly displayed about 4.2 kW. Its sampling interval and AC/DC basis still need documenting before that instantaneous value can be interpreted confidently. It is not used in the monthly-energy or per-kWp calculations above.

Common questions

Solar quality, cost and generation — quick answers

Why can a cheap solar system cost more in the long run?

A lower installation price must be weighed against the electricity the system produces over time, equipment quality, warranties and service. If generation is lower, the system can offset less grid electricity and the upfront saving can be eroded. Our September same-roof records show 529.13 kWh from the premium TOPCon array versus 385.4 kWh from the tested budget Mono PERC array. Lifetime cost still depends on the actual quotations, tariffs, consumption, maintenance and future generation; these records are not a lifetime savings calculation.

Can high-quality Mono PERC perform on par with TOPCon?

High-quality Mono PERC can be a competitive choice alongside newer TOPCon. The Tata installation in Alwar City, with six 590 W Mono PERC panels, generated 521.1 kWh from 9 September to 8 October 2026, averaging 17.4 kWh/day. The six-panel 610 W TOPCon installation in Behror generated 529.13 kWh from 1 to 30 September 2026, averaging 17.64 kWh/day. Both show strong recorded output, but their locations, periods and DC capacities differ, so these examples do not establish a matched technology ranking.

How much more did TOPCon generate than the budget Mono PERC system?

From 1 to 30 September 2026, the 3.66 kWp TOPCon array generated 529.13 kWh and the 3.18 kWp budget Mono PERC array generated 385.4 kWh on the same roof. The raw difference was 143.73 kWh, or 37.3% more energy. Dividing by rated DC capacity gives 144.57 versus 121.19 kWh/kWp, an observed 19.3% difference per installed DC kWp. These were separate-inverter installations, so the result does not isolate cell technology as the sole cause or establish an annual advantage.

Does TOPCon always beat Mono PERC?

No. The tested budget 530 W Mono PERC array is different from the high-quality Tata 590 W Mono PERC example. The same-roof TOPCon array recorded 19.3% more energy per rated DC kWp over September 2026, with earlier observed differences of 16.9% on 21 August and 17.4% on 27 August. Those results belong to the tested arrays and periods. Compare exact module models, configuration, installation quality, warranties and service rather than assuming one technology label always wins.

Are all three records from the same roof and dates?

The TOPCon and budget Mono PERC records are from the same two Behror rooftop arrays, observed on 21 and 27 August and over 1–30 September 2026. The Tata Mono PERC record is from Alwar City over 9 September–8 October 2026. It is a separate real-world example, not a third array in the same-roof comparison.

Is TOPCon eligible for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?

Yes — when it's a DCR (made-in-India) TOPCon module. We fit DCR-certified TOPCon panels that qualify for the full PM Surya Ghar subsidy, the same as any eligible system.
